Workers compensation for industrial coatings contractors on major infrastructure and facility projects is more complex than most insurance lines. The injury patterns are specific, the regulatory environment is demanding, and the carrier selection can significantly affect both your premium and how claims are handled.

This guide covers what workers comp covers for industrial coatings crews, the most significant claim types, and how to structure your program for major project work.

## The Major Injury Categories for Industrial Coatings Crews

### Falls from Elevation

Falls are the leading cause of serious injury and fatality in industrial coatings work. Bridge painting crews work on swing stages, scaffolding, and aerial lifts at heights from 20 feet to 200 feet or more. Facility coatings crews work on scaffolding in process areas, on elevated structure maintenance platforms, and on process towers and tanks.

A single fall from elevation can result in:
- Fatal injury (the most catastrophic workers comp claim)
- Catastrophic injury — traumatic brain injury, spinal cord injury, multiple fractures
- Serious injury — fractures, lacerations, soft tissue damage requiring extended rehabilitation

Workers comp covers all medical costs and wage replacement for fall injuries, regardless of how they occurred. From a risk management perspective, fall protection — OSHA-compliant equipment, training, and supervision — is the most important loss control measure in industrial coatings.

### Lead Exposure

Abrasive blasting on bridges and older industrial structures frequently disturbs lead-based paint. OSHA's Lead Standard for Construction (29 CFR 1926.62) establishes action levels, permissible exposure limits, medical monitoring requirements, and work practice controls for lead exposure.

Despite regulatory requirements, lead exposure injuries occur in industrial coatings:
- Blood lead elevation above the medical removal level triggers restricted duty or medical removal
- Long-term elevated blood lead levels cause neurological effects, cognitive impairment, and other systemic health effects
- Occupational lead toxicity can be permanent, creating long-duration workers comp claims

Workers comp covers:
- Medical monitoring and treatment for elevated blood lead
- Wage replacement during medical removal
- Long-term care and disability benefits for workers with permanent lead-related health effects

Carrier underwriting for lead exposure work varies significantly. Carriers familiar with bridge painting and lead paint abatement underwrite it more accurately than carriers applying general painting classifications.

### Chemical Exposure — Occupational Disease

Industrial coatings work exposes crews to a range of chemical hazards:

**Isocyanate exposure.** Polyurethane topcoat systems use isocyanate hardeners — among the most potent respiratory sensitizers in industrial use. Once sensitized, a worker may be unable to work around isocyanates at all. Occupational asthma from isocyanate sensitization is a significant workers comp claim type in industrial coatings.

**Amine vapor exposure.** Epoxy hardeners release amine vapors during mixing and application. Amines cause skin sensitization, respiratory irritation, and occupational asthma with repeated exposure.

**Solvent exposure.** Solvent-based coating systems — common in industrial coatings for performance reasons — create VOC exposure risks including respiratory irritation, central nervous system effects from chronic exposure, and skin sensitization.

**Zinc dust exposure.** Zinc-rich primer application creates zinc dust exposure. Zinc fever — flu-like symptoms from acute zinc dust exposure — is a documented occupational condition in industrial coatings work.

Workers comp covers occupational diseases that develop from these exposures — medical treatment, wage replacement, and disability benefits when chemical exposure causes permanent impairment.

### Confined Space Incidents

Tank coatings, vessel interior coatings, and other confined space coating work creates specific hazards:

**Oxygen deficiency.** Coating materials in enclosed spaces consume oxygen during curing. Solvent evaporation can create flammable or toxic atmospheres. Oxygen deficiency can cause sudden incapacitation and death.

**Toxic atmosphere.** Solvent vapors from coating materials reach dangerous concentrations more quickly in confined spaces than in open environments.

**Physical entrapment.** Confined space geometry creates entrapment risk during emergency egress.

OSHA's Permit-Required Confined Space standard (29 CFR 1926.1200 for construction) requires atmospheric testing, ventilation, attendant supervision, and rescue procedures for confined space coating work. Workers comp covers injuries and illnesses that occur in confined spaces despite these controls.

### Musculoskeletal Injuries

Industrial coatings work creates sustained musculoskeletal stress:
- Blast gun operation creates vibration exposure and upper extremity stress
- Extended overhead work during structure and ceiling coatings stresses shoulders and cervical spine
- Scaffolding assembly and disassembly involves heavy lifting and awkward postures
- Spray application equipment handling creates back and shoulder strain

Musculoskeletal injuries are frequent in industrial coatings and, while typically less severe than falls or chemical exposure, accumulate over a workforce and increase the experience modification factor if not managed through ergonomics and early return-to-work programs.

## Class Codes and Premium Calculation

Workers compensation premiums for industrial coatings contractors depend on:

1. **Class code assignment.** The code that applies to your work determines the base rate. Bridge painting, industrial facility painting, and specialty structural coatings may carry different codes in different states — and the rates for those codes vary significantly.

2. **Payroll.** Premium is calculated as (payroll / 100) × rate. More employees and higher wages mean higher premium.

3. **Experience modification factor (EMF or MOD).** Your claims history relative to similar contractors. A MOD below 1.0 means a discount; above 1.0 means a surcharge. Major injury claims — fall fatalities, lead toxicity, occupational disease — can significantly increase your MOD for 3 years.

4. **State.** Workers comp is state-regulated. Rates, codes, and benefit levels vary by state. Multi-state coatings contractors may have dramatically different WC costs per employee depending on which state they're working in.

Getting the class code right matters. Incorrect classification — either direction — creates premium errors that are reconciled at audit with potentially significant consequences.

## Multi-State Workers Compensation Programs

Industrial coatings contractors frequently work in multiple states. Workers compensation is state-regulated, which means:

- Each state has its own codes, rates, and benefit structures
- Some states (Ohio, Wyoming, Washington, North Dakota) have monopolistic state funds — private insurance is not an option
- Employees working primarily in one state are covered under that state's workers comp, but coverage extends to incidental work in other states
- Contractors with employees regularly assigned to multiple states may need multi-state policies
