Industrial facility coatings work — refineries, chemical plants, manufacturing facilities, wastewater treatment plants, power generation facilities — represents one of the most complex insurance environments for coatings contractors. The work environments are hazardous, the liability exposure is significant, and the insurance requirements imposed by facility owners often exceed what standard contractor programs provide.

This guide covers the specific insurance considerations for industrial coatings contractors working in facility environments.

## The Industrial Facility Coatings Risk Profile

Industrial facility coatings work differs from bridge painting or floor coating in several important ways:

**Hazardous operating environments.** Coatings work in refineries and chemical plants occurs in environments with process hazards — flammable vapors, toxic process materials, hot work restrictions, confined space entry requirements, and the constant presence of operating process equipment. These environments create elevated risk for both workers and third parties.

**High operator insurance requirements.** Petrochemical operators, major manufacturers, and major facility owners impose detailed insurance requirements on all contractors entering their facilities. These typically include: minimum GL and umbrella limits (often $5M to $10M total), specific AI endorsement forms, CPL requirements, contractors insurance certificates before mobilization, and ongoing certificate maintenance throughout the project.

**Chemical complexity.** Industrial facility coatings often involve specialty systems — chemical-resistant coatings for containment structures, high-temperature coatings for process equipment, zinc-rich primer systems for structural steel, and specialty coatings for specific process environments. The chemical complexity increases both worker exposure and professional liability risk.

**Long-duration projects.** Major facility maintenance coatings projects — a refinery turnaround, a tank farm re-coating program, a structural steel maintenance cycle — can run months to years. Long project duration increases the cumulative exposure for both liability and workers comp.

**Process continuity implications.** When coatings work disrupts facility operations — fume migration into occupied areas, containment failures affecting process equipment, or coating failures affecting critical infrastructure — the resulting claims can include production loss and business interruption costs that exceed the original contract value.

## GL for Industrial Facility Coatings Contractors

GL for industrial facility coatings contractors must address several specific issues:

**Facility operator AI requirements.** Major operators require specific AI language — primary and non-contributory, specific endorsement forms, waiver of subrogation. We build these requirements into your policy so your certificates satisfy facility owner requirements before you mobilize.

**Completed operations for coating systems with performance specifications.** Chemical-resistant linings, high-temperature coatings, and specialty facility coatings that have performance specifications create completed operations exposure beyond the immediate project period. Your GL completed operations coverage should extend for a meaningful period after project completion.

**Property damage coverage in operating facilities.** Coatings work in operating facilities creates property damage exposure that is different from new construction — damage to existing process equipment, piping, instrumentation, and infrastructure. Make sure your GL is not limited in ways that exclude property damage in complex operating environments.

## CPL for Petrochemical and Chemical Plant Coatings

Contractors pollution liability is especially important for industrial facility coatings work. The environments where you work are themselves classified as industrial and potentially polluting — adding your coating materials to that environment creates combined pollution risk:

**VOC and solvent emissions in process environments.** Solvent-based coatings applied in process areas create VOC emissions that interact with process atmospheres. In facilities with explosion risk or strict air quality permits, coating solvent emissions can create liability exposure.

**Coating material releases in containment-critical areas.** Spills of coating materials in secondary containment areas, near storm drains, or in areas with environmental sensitivity create cleanup requirements and potential claims.

**Fume migration into occupied facility areas.** Major facilities have occupied areas — control rooms, administrative buildings, laboratory spaces — adjacent to coatings work areas. Fume migration into these areas creates third-party bodily injury exposure that GL may exclude and CPL covers.

**Chemical exposure to facility workers.** Coatings work occurring while a facility is partially operating creates chemical exposure risk for facility workers who are not part of your crew. CPL covers bodily injury claims from these third parties.

For industrial facility coatings contractors, CPL limits should be calibrated to the scale and sensitivity of your facility work. Operations at major refineries or chemical plants warrant higher limits than standard floor coating or facility maintenance work.

## Professional Liability for Facility Coatings

Specialty coatings applied in industrial facilities often carry performance obligations that create professional liability exposure:

**Chemical resistance specifications.** A coating specified as resistant to specific process chemicals in a containment area must actually resist those chemicals. If the coating fails to resist the specified chemicals and a spill occurs — resulting in environmental contamination or process facility damage — the resulting claim may include the contractor who specified or applied the coating.

**High-temperature coating performance.** Coatings on process piping, boilers, and high-temperature equipment are specified to function at specific temperatures. Failure at temperatures within the specification range creates professional liability exposure.

**Structural coating for critical infrastructure.** Anti-corrosion coatings on structural elements of operating facilities — process vessel supports, pipe racks, crane runways — have safety implications if the coating fails to protect the structure within the specified period.

**Containment lining failures.** Secondary containment linings in chemical facilities are often life-of-facility specifications. A lining failure in a primary containment area can result in environmental release with regulatory and remediation consequences far exceeding the original lining cost.

For industrial facility coatings contractors, professional liability limits should reflect the potential cost of coating system failures in the specific environments where you work.

## Umbrella Limits for Facility Work

Industrial facility operators routinely require higher total liability limits than most other project owners. Common requirements:

- Major manufacturer facility coatings: $5M total liability
- Petrochemical plant and refinery work: $5M to $10M total
- Chemical plant coatings in regulated environments: may require $15M or more

An umbrella policy provides the most cost-effective path to these limits. We include umbrella capacity in every industrial facility coatings program proposal and can access excess layers for the largest capacity requirements.

## Workers Compensation in Industrial Environments

Workers compensation for coatings crews working in operating industrial facilities covers:

**Chemical exposure to process materials.** Your coating crews may be exposed not only to coating materials but also to the facility's process chemicals. Workers comp covers occupational disease and injury from these combined exposures.

**Hot work and ignition hazards.** Coatings work near process areas may require hot work permits and strict ignition control. Workers comp covers injuries from fire and explosion events, but OSHA compliance and permit compliance are the primary risk controls.

**Confined space entry.** Tank, vessel, and confined space coatings work in industrial facilities involves confined space entry procedures. Oxygen deficiency, toxic atmosphere, and physical entrapment risks are covered by workers comp when injuries occur.

**Elevated work in complex environments.** Coatings work on elevated structures in operating facilities — pipe racks, process towers, elevated structures — combines height risk with the complexity of operating equipment below and around the work area.

Carrier selection for industrial facility coatings workers comp matters. Carriers familiar with industrial facility environments understand the risk profile and underwrite it more accurately than carriers applying general painting classifications.
